The Respiratory System

Learn how air enters the body, how oxygen reaches the blood, how carbon dioxide is removed, and why respiration is essential during pregnancy and childbirth.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Define the respiratory system.
  • Identify the major respiratory organs.
  • Explain the pathway of air through the respiratory tract.
  • Describe the role of the alveoli in gas exchange.
  • Explain the difference between ventilation and gas exchange.
  • Describe important respiratory changes during pregnancy.
  • Explain the importance of oxygenation during labour.

1. What is the Respiratory System?

The respiratory system is responsible for bringing oxygen into the body and removing carbon dioxide.

Oxygen is essential for cellular metabolism, while carbon dioxide is a major waste product that must be removed.

Simple definition:

The respiratory system allows the body to take in oxygen and eliminate carbon dioxide.

2. Major Organs of the Respiratory System

👃

Nose and Nasal Cavity

Help filter, warm and humidify incoming air.

👄

Pharynx

A passage that connects the nasal and oral cavities with the larynx.

🗣️

Larynx

Connects the pharynx with the trachea and contains the vocal cords.

🫁

Trachea

Conducts air toward the bronchi and lungs.

🌿

Bronchi

The trachea divides into right and left main bronchi that enter the lungs.

🫧

Alveoli

Tiny air sacs where oxygen and carbon dioxide are exchanged between air and blood.

3. Pathway of Air

Nose / Mouth ↓ Pharynx ↓ Larynx ↓ Trachea ↓ Bronchi ↓ Bronchioles ↓ Alveoli

4. Ventilation

Ventilation is the movement of air into and out of the lungs.

Inspiration

Inspiration is breathing air into the lungs. The diaphragm contracts and moves downward, helping increase the volume of the thoracic cavity.

Expiration

Expiration is breathing air out of the lungs. During quiet breathing, it is largely passive.

🧠 Remember

Inspiration = air moves into the lungs.

Expiration = air moves out of the lungs.

5. The Diaphragm

The diaphragm is the major muscle of respiration. It separates the thoracic cavity from the abdominal cavity.

When the diaphragm contracts, it moves downward and helps draw air into the lungs.

🩺 Clinical connection

Changes in the position and movement of the diaphragm contribute to the altered respiratory experience commonly seen during pregnancy.

6. Gas Exchange

Gas exchange occurs mainly across the thin walls of the alveoli and surrounding pulmonary capillaries.

🫁

Oxygen

Moves from the alveolar air into the blood.

♻️

Carbon Dioxide

Moves from the blood into the alveoli and is then exhaled.

7. Oxygen Transport

Most oxygen in blood is transported bound to hemoglobin inside red blood cells.

Oxygenated blood is delivered through the circulation to tissues where oxygen is released for cellular use.

Key idea:

Lungs oxygenate blood → cardiovascular system transports oxygen → tissues use oxygen.

8. Respiratory Rate

Respiratory rate is the number of breaths taken in one minute.

Respiratory rate is one of the vital signs assessed during clinical care.

🩺 Midwifery connection

Assess respiratory rate and overall breathing whenever maternal condition requires clinical assessment, particularly when there are signs of respiratory distress or illness.

9. Respiratory Changes During Pregnancy

Pregnancy produces physiological changes in the respiratory system to support increased maternal metabolic needs and fetal development.

Increased Oxygen Demand

Maternal oxygen consumption increases during pregnancy.

Diaphragm Position

As the uterus enlarges, the diaphragm is displaced upward.

Increased Ventilation

Maternal ventilation increases, particularly through an increase in tidal volume.

Hormonal Effects

Pregnancy hormones contribute to changes in respiratory physiology and breathing sensations.

10. Maternal and Fetal Oxygenation

The fetus depends on the placenta for exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide between maternal and fetal circulations.

Mother ↓ Maternal Blood ↓ Placenta ↓ Fetal Circulation ↓ Fetus
🤰 Important midwifery point

Adequate maternal oxygenation and circulation are important for maintaining oxygen delivery to the fetus.

11. Respiration During Labour

Labour increases physical and metabolic demands. Breathing patterns may change as contractions become stronger and more frequent.

Appropriate supportive care can help a woman remain comfortable and cope with labour.

🩺 Midwifery practice

Observe the woman's breathing, overall condition, oxygenation when clinically indicated, and signs of respiratory distress.

12. Signs of Respiratory Distress

Respiratory distress may present with symptoms and signs such as:

  • Difficulty breathing
  • Very rapid or unusually slow breathing
  • Increased work of breathing
  • Cyanosis or abnormal skin/lip coloration
  • Altered level of consciousness in severe cases
⚠️ Clinical warning

Significant breathing difficulty or suspected hypoxia requires prompt clinical assessment and escalation according to local emergency protocols.

13. Summary

🫁 Remember

Air enters → Airways → Alveoli

Alveoli → Oxygen enters blood

Blood → Oxygen delivered to tissues

Carbon dioxide → Blood → Alveoli → Exhaled

Pregnancy → Increased respiratory demand

🧠 Quick Review

1. What is the main function of the respiratory system?
To bring oxygen into the body and remove carbon dioxide.
2. Where does most gas exchange occur?
At the alveoli and surrounding pulmonary capillaries.
3. What is the main muscle of respiration?
The diaphragm.
4. What is inspiration?
Movement of air into the lungs.
5. What is expiration?
Movement of air out of the lungs.
6. Why is maternal respiration important during pregnancy?
Maternal respiration supplies oxygen and removes carbon dioxide, supporting maternal metabolism and oxygen delivery to the fetus through placental exchange.
