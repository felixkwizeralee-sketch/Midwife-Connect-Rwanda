Pregnancy & Fetal Development

Understand what happens from fertilization and implantation through fetal development, placental function and the major physiological changes of pregnancy.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain the sequence from fertilization to implantation.
  • Distinguish between embryonic and fetal development.
  • Describe major fetal developmental changes.
  • Explain the role of the placenta and umbilical cord.
  • Describe fetal circulation at a basic level.
  • Explain the three trimesters of pregnancy.
  • Identify important maternal physiological adaptations.
  • Recognize important warning signs that require clinical assessment.

1. What is Pregnancy?

Pregnancy is the physiological state in which a developing embryo and fetus grow within the uterus following successful implantation.

Simple definition:

Pregnancy begins after implantation and involves coordinated changes in the mother, placenta and developing fetus.

2. From Fertilization to Implantation

After fertilization, the developing conceptus undergoes cell division and travels through the uterine tube toward the uterus.

Fertilization → Zygote → Cleavage → Morula → Blastocyst → Implantation

Zygote

The zygote is the single cell formed when the sperm and ovum unite.

Morula

The morula is an early solid ball of cells formed through cell division.

Blastocyst

The blastocyst is a later stage that contains an inner cell mass and an outer cell layer. It is the stage that implants into the uterine lining.

3. Implantation

Implantation occurs when the developing blastocyst attaches to and becomes embedded in the endometrium of the uterus.

🧠 Important

Fertilization usually occurs in the uterine tube, while implantation occurs in the uterine endometrium.

4. Embryo and Fetus

🧬 Embryonic Period

The embryonic period extends from approximately the third through the eighth week after fertilization. During this period, major body structures and organ systems begin to form.

👶 Fetal Period

The fetal period begins around the ninth week after fertilization and continues until birth. Growth and maturation of established organs become major features.

5. The Three Trimesters

Pregnancy is commonly divided into three trimesters. Exact week boundaries can vary slightly depending on the clinical convention being used.

🌱

First Trimester

Early pregnancy is characterized by implantation, development of the placenta and major embryonic development. Organ formation is particularly important during this period.

🌿

Second Trimester

Fetal growth becomes prominent. Body proportions change, movements become more noticeable and anatomical structures continue to mature.

🌳

Third Trimester

Rapid fetal growth and maturation continue. The fetus prepares physiologically for life outside the uterus.

6. Major Fetal Development

Fetal development is continuous. The following milestones provide a simplified learning guide rather than an exact prediction of every fetal change.

Weeks 1–4

Fertilization, early cell division, movement toward the uterus and implantation occur. Early embryonic structures begin developing.

Weeks 5–8

Major organ systems begin forming. The neural tube, heart and limb structures undergo important early development.

Weeks 9–12

The developing baby is in the fetal period. Growth continues and many external features become increasingly recognizable.

Weeks 13–20

The fetus grows substantially. Skeletal development, movement and continued maturation occur.

Weeks 21–28

The nervous system, lungs and other organs continue to mature. Fetal movements become increasingly coordinated.

Weeks 29–36

Fetal growth and maturation continue, including important development of the lungs and nervous system.

Weeks 37–40+

The fetus continues final maturation and growth. The pregnancy progresses toward labour and birth.

7. The Placenta

The placenta is a temporary organ that develops during pregnancy and performs several essential functions.

🫁 Gas Exchange

Oxygen moves from maternal blood toward the fetal circulation while carbon dioxide moves in the opposite direction.

🍎 Nutrient Transfer

Nutrients and other substances can move from the maternal circulation to the fetus.

🗑️ Waste Removal

Metabolic waste products from the fetus can be transferred toward the maternal circulation.

🧪 Hormone Production

The placenta produces hormones that support pregnancy and maternal adaptation.

🩺 Midwifery connection

Placental function is central to fetal wellbeing. Abnormal placental function can contribute to fetal growth problems and other pregnancy complications.

8. Umbilical Cord

The umbilical cord connects the fetus to the placenta and contains blood vessels that transport blood between them.

Remember:

A typical umbilical cord contains two arteries and one vein.

The umbilical vein carries relatively oxygen-rich blood from the placenta toward the fetus. The umbilical arteries carry relatively oxygen-poor blood from the fetus toward the placenta.

9. Amniotic Fluid

Amniotic fluid surrounds the fetus within the amniotic sac. It provides a protective environment for fetal development.

🛡️ Protection

Helps cushion the fetus from external mechanical forces.

🦴 Movement

Allows fetal movement, which supports normal musculoskeletal development.

🌡️ Environment

Helps maintain a suitable environment around the developing fetus.

🫁 Development

Fetal breathing movements and interaction with amniotic fluid contribute to normal lung development.

10. Fetal Circulation

Before birth, the fetus does not obtain oxygen through its lungs. Oxygenated blood comes from the placenta.

Placenta → Umbilical vein → Fetal circulation → Umbilical arteries → Placenta

Important fetal shunts

Foramen Ovale

Allows blood to pass from the right atrium toward the left atrium before birth.

Ductus Arteriosus

Provides a connection between the pulmonary artery and aorta, helping divert blood away from the fetal lungs.

Ductus Venosus

Allows a portion of oxygen-rich blood from the umbilical vein to bypass much of the liver.

11. Changes at Birth

At birth, the newborn begins breathing air and the circulation changes rapidly.

First breaths → Lung expansion → Increased pulmonary blood flow → Fetal shunts begin to close functionally
👶 Important

The transition from fetal to newborn circulation is a major physiological change that occurs after birth.

12. Maternal Physiological Changes

Pregnancy affects almost every maternal body system. These changes help support the growing fetus and prepare the mother for labour and breastfeeding.

❤️ Cardiovascular

Blood volume and cardiac output increase during pregnancy, with important adaptations in the cardiovascular system.

🫁 Respiratory

Ventilation increases and the growing uterus also influences respiratory mechanics.

🧪 Endocrine

Placental and maternal hormones regulate many changes associated with pregnancy.

🫘 Renal

Renal blood flow and glomerular filtration increase during normal pregnancy.

13. Fetal Movement

Fetal movement is an important part of pregnancy assessment. The mother may begin to perceive movement during the second trimester, although the timing varies.

🩺 Midwifery connection

A change or significant reduction in fetal movements reported by a pregnant woman requires appropriate assessment according to local clinical protocols.

14. Fetal Growth and Wellbeing

Fetal growth is influenced by genetic, maternal, placental and environmental factors.

Maternal Factors

Maternal nutrition, health conditions, infections, medications and other factors can influence pregnancy and fetal growth.

Placental Factors

Adequate placental function is important for oxygen and nutrient transfer.

Fetal Factors

Genetic and developmental factors contribute to individual differences in fetal growth.

Environmental Factors

Exposure to harmful substances and other environmental influences can affect fetal health.

15. Why This Matters in Midwifery

🤰 Midwife Connect Rwanda

Understanding pregnancy and fetal development helps the midwife provide quality antenatal education, monitor maternal and fetal wellbeing, recognize deviations from normal pregnancy and refer clients appropriately when concerns arise.

16. Important Pregnancy Warning Signs

A pregnant woman should receive prompt clinical assessment when concerning symptoms occur.

  • Vaginal bleeding during pregnancy.
  • Severe or persistent abdominal pain.
  • Severe headache, visual disturbance or other symptoms concerning for hypertensive disease.
  • Convulsions or loss of consciousness.
  • Difficulty breathing or severe chest symptoms.
  • Leakage of fluid from the vagina when preterm rupture of membranes is suspected.
  • Reduced or absent fetal movement after movements have been established.
  • Fever or other symptoms suggesting serious infection.
⚠️ Clinical warning

Pregnancy warning signs require clinical assessment. This educational lesson does not replace examination, diagnostic testing, referral or emergency care.

17. Summary

🧠 Remember

Fertilization usually occurs in the uterine tube.

Implantation occurs in the uterine endometrium.

The embryonic period is especially important for formation of major body structures.

The fetal period is characterized mainly by growth and maturation.

The placenta performs exchange and endocrine functions.

A typical umbilical cord contains two arteries and one vein.

Fetal circulation uses special pathways because the fetal lungs are not yet functioning for gas exchange.

Pregnancy produces major physiological changes throughout the maternal body.

🧠 Quick Review

1. Where does fertilization usually occur?
In the uterine tube, commonly in the ampulla.
2. Where does implantation occur?
In the endometrium of the uterus.
3. When does the fetal period begin?
Around the ninth week after fertilization.
4. What are the main functions of the placenta?
It supports gas and nutrient exchange, waste transfer, endocrine functions and other aspects of pregnancy support.
5. How many arteries and veins are normally found in the umbilical cord?
Two arteries and one vein.
6. Why does fetal circulation have special shunts?
Because before birth the fetal lungs are not responsible for gas exchange.
7. Name the three trimesters of pregnancy.
First trimester, second trimester and third trimester.
8. Why is fetal movement clinically important?
Changes in fetal movement can provide an important indication that further fetal assessment may be needed.
9. Name two important pregnancy warning signs.
Examples include vaginal bleeding and severe abdominal pain. Other serious warning signs require prompt clinical assessment.
