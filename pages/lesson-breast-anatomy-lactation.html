Breast Anatomy & Lactation

Understand breast anatomy, milk production, hormonal control of lactation, breastfeeding physiology, colostrum, milk ejection and essential midwifery care.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Describe the basic anatomy of the breast.
  • Explain the function of the mammary glands.
  • Describe the process of milk production.
  • Explain the roles of prolactin and oxytocin.
  • Distinguish colostrum, transitional milk and mature breast milk.
  • Explain the milk ejection reflex.
  • Describe basic principles of effective breastfeeding.
  • Recognize common breastfeeding difficulties and warning signs.
  • Explain the role of the midwife in breastfeeding support.

1. What is Lactation?

Lactation is the physiological process of producing and secreting breast milk after childbirth.

Simple definition:

Lactation is the production and release of breast milk from the mammary glands to nourish the newborn.

2. Breast Anatomy

The breast contains glandular tissue, ducts, connective tissue and adipose tissue. The mammary gland is specialized for milk production.

🧬

Mammary Glands

Specialized glandular tissue responsible for producing breast milk.

🫧

Alveoli

Small milk-producing structures lined by secretory cells. Milk is produced within the alveolar units.

➡️

Lactiferous Ducts

Ducts that transport milk from the glandular tissue toward the nipple.

🌸

Nipple

The nipple contains openings through which breast milk is delivered to the infant.

🟤

Areola

The pigmented area surrounding the nipple. It contains glands that help maintain the skin and contribute to nipple preparation during breastfeeding.

🧈

Adipose Tissue

Fatty tissue contributes to the size and shape of the breast and surrounds glandular structures.

3. How Breast Milk is Produced

Milk production is regulated by hormonal changes and stimulation of the breast by the infant.

Baby suckles → Nipple stimulation → Hormonal response → Milk production & ejection

4. Hormonal Control of Lactation

🧪 Prolactin

Prolactin stimulates the mammary glands to produce breast milk.

Frequent and effective breast stimulation helps maintain milk production.

💗 Oxytocin

Oxytocin causes contraction of myoepithelial cells around the alveoli, helping milk move toward the ducts and nipple.

This is known as the milk ejection or let-down reflex.

🧠 Remember

Prolactin = milk production.
Oxytocin = milk ejection.

5. Milk Ejection Reflex

When the baby suckles, sensory receptors around the nipple are stimulated. This stimulates oxytocin release from the posterior pituitary.

Suckling → Nipple stimulation → Oxytocin release → Alveolar contraction → Milk ejection
🩺 Midwifery connection

Stress, pain, fear and anxiety can interfere with the mother's breastfeeding experience and may affect milk ejection. Emotional support and a calm environment can be helpful.

6. Colostrum

Colostrum is the first breast milk produced during the early days after birth.

🟡 Appearance

Colostrum is often yellowish or golden in appearance.

🛡️ Immune Protection

It contains important immune components that help protect the newborn.

💪 Nutritional Value

It provides concentrated nutrients and bioactive substances appropriate for the newborn.

👶 Small Amounts

It is produced in relatively small quantities, which are generally appropriate for the newborn's early stomach capacity.

7. Types of Breast Milk

🟡 Colostrum

Early milk produced after birth. It is rich in protective factors and nutrients.

🌱 Transitional Milk

Milk produced during the transition from colostrum toward mature breast milk.

🥛 Mature Milk

Established breast milk that provides energy, nutrients, fluid and bioactive components for the growing infant.

8. Changes During a Breastfeed

Milk composition changes during a feeding. The terms "foremilk" and "hindmilk" are commonly used to describe milk at different stages of a feed, but breast milk is continuously changing rather than being two completely separate types.

💡 Practical point

Allowing the infant to feed effectively and adequately from the breast helps the infant receive the available milk and supports effective milk removal.

9. Initiating Breastfeeding

Early support after birth can help establish effective breastfeeding.

Skin-to-skin contact

When clinically appropriate, place the newborn skin-to-skin with the mother soon after birth.

Early breastfeeding

Support breastfeeding as soon as the mother and baby are ready and clinically stable.

Correct positioning

Help the mother position the baby close to her body with the baby's head and body aligned.

Effective attachment

Observe the baby's attachment and sucking and correct problems early.

Frequent feeding

Encourage responsive feeding and avoid unnecessary restrictions on breastfeeding frequency.

10. Breastfeeding Positioning

Good positioning helps the infant attach effectively and can reduce nipple pain and improve milk transfer.

👶 Baby Close to Mother

The baby's body should be close to the mother rather than reaching toward the breast.

👂 Head and Body Aligned

The baby's head, neck and body should be comfortably aligned.

👄 Wide Mouth

Encourage a wide mouth before bringing the baby onto the breast.

🤱 Deep Attachment

The baby should take a substantial portion of the breast into the mouth, not just the nipple.

11. Signs of Effective Breastfeeding

👄 Good Attachment

The infant appears well attached and suckling is effective.

😊 Comfortable Mother

Breastfeeding should not cause persistent severe nipple pain when attachment is effective.

👂 Swallowing

Swallowing can be observed or heard during effective milk transfer.

👶 Satisfied Infant

The baby may appear satisfied after an effective feed, although feeding behavior varies between newborns.

12. Exclusive Breastfeeding

Exclusive breastfeeding means giving an infant only breast milk, except for medicines, vitamins, minerals or oral rehydration solution when medically indicated.

🍼 Key recommendation

WHO recommends exclusive breastfeeding for the first 6 months of life when possible and appropriate, followed by continued breastfeeding with complementary foods.

13. Benefits of Breastfeeding

👶

Benefits for the Baby

Breast milk provides nutrients, immune protection and bioactive components important for infant health and development.

🤱

Benefits for the Mother

Breastfeeding supports postpartum physiological processes and provides important maternal and emotional benefits.

🌍

Family Benefits

Breastfeeding is readily available and can reduce the cost and preparation required for infant feeding.

❤️

Bonding

Close contact during breastfeeding can support bonding and responsive interaction between mother and baby.

14. Common Breastfeeding Problems

😣 Nipple Pain

Often associated with poor positioning or attachment. The mother should be assessed and supported.

🩷 Engorgement

Breasts may become swollen, firm and uncomfortable. Effective milk removal and appropriate support are important.

🔴 Mastitis

Breast inflammation may cause pain, redness, swelling and sometimes fever. Clinical assessment may be required.

💧 Perceived Low Milk Supply

Assessment should consider feeding frequency, attachment, milk transfer, infant output and growth rather than assuming insufficient milk.

15. Role of the Midwife

Assess breastfeeding

Observe positioning, attachment, sucking and maternal comfort.

Provide education

Explain breastfeeding physiology and answer the mother's questions.

Correct problems

Help improve positioning and attachment when needed.

Support confidence

Provide respectful, supportive and non-judgmental care.

Refer when necessary

Identify problems that require additional clinical assessment or specialist support.

16. Breastfeeding Warning Signs

Some maternal or newborn conditions require prompt assessment.

  • Severe or worsening breast pain.
  • Significant breast redness or swelling with fever.
  • Persistent nipple injury or bleeding.
  • Newborn difficulty feeding or repeatedly refusing feeds.
  • Signs of dehydration or inadequate intake in the newborn.
  • Poor newborn weight gain requiring assessment.
  • Maternal illness or medication concerns affecting breastfeeding.
⚠️ Clinical warning

Breastfeeding problems should be assessed individually. Do not advise a mother to stop breastfeeding routinely without identifying the clinical reason and considering appropriate alternatives.

17. Summary

🧠 Remember

The mammary glands produce breast milk.

Alveoli are important milk-producing structures.

Lactiferous ducts transport milk toward the nipple.

Prolactin is primarily associated with milk production.

Oxytocin is primarily associated with milk ejection.

Colostrum is the first milk produced after birth.

Good positioning and attachment support effective breastfeeding.

Exclusive breastfeeding is recommended for the first 6 months when possible and appropriate.

Midwives play an important role in breastfeeding education, assessment and support.

🧠 Quick Review

1. What is lactation?
Lactation is the production and secretion of breast milk by the mammary glands.
2. What are the milk-producing structures of the breast?
The alveoli are important milk-producing structures.
3. What is the role of prolactin?
Prolactin stimulates breast milk production.
4. What is the role of oxytocin?
Oxytocin causes contraction of myoepithelial cells, helping eject milk toward the nipple.
5. What is colostrum?
Colostrum is the first breast milk produced during the early days after birth.
6. What is exclusive breastfeeding?
Feeding the infant only breast milk, with specified medical exceptions such as medicines or oral rehydration solution when indicated.
7. What can cause nipple pain during breastfeeding?
Poor positioning or ineffective attachment is a common cause and should be assessed.
8. Name two responsibilities of a midwife in breastfeeding support.
Examples include assessing attachment, providing breastfeeding education, correcting positioning problems and identifying cases requiring referral.
9. For how long does WHO recommend exclusive breastfeeding?
For the first 6 months of life when possible and appropriate.
