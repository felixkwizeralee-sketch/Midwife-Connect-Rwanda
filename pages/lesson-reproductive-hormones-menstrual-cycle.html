Reproductive Hormones & the Menstrual Cycle

Learn how the hypothalamus, pituitary gland and ovaries coordinate reproductive hormones, ovulation and the menstrual cycle.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis.
  • Describe the functions of GnRH, FSH, LH, estrogen and progesterone.
  • Describe the main phases of the menstrual cycle.
  • Explain follicular development and ovulation.
  • Explain the role of the corpus luteum.
  • Describe hormonal feedback during the cycle.
  • Explain the relationship between ovulation, fertilization and pregnancy.
  • Connect menstrual-cycle physiology to midwifery care.

1. What is the Menstrual Cycle?

The menstrual cycle is a repeating sequence of hormonal and reproductive changes that prepares the female reproductive system for possible pregnancy.

Simple definition:

The menstrual cycle involves coordinated changes in the ovaries and uterus controlled by reproductive hormones.

Cycle length varies between individuals and from cycle to cycle. A commonly used average is approximately 28 days, but normal cycles can be shorter or longer.

2. The Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Ovarian Axis

Reproductive function is regulated through communication between the hypothalamus, pituitary gland and ovaries.

Hypothalamus → GnRH → Anterior Pituitary → FSH + LH → Ovaries → Estrogen + Progesterone

3. Major Reproductive Hormones

🧠

GnRH

Gonadotropin-releasing hormone is produced by the hypothalamus. It stimulates the anterior pituitary to release FSH and LH.

🌱

FSH

Follicle-stimulating hormone promotes development of ovarian follicles and supports estrogen production by the developing follicle.

⚡

LH

Luteinizing hormone plays a major role in triggering ovulation and supports formation and function of the corpus luteum.

🌸

Estrogen

Estrogen is produced mainly by developing ovarian follicles during the follicular phase. It promotes growth of the endometrium and participates in feedback regulation.

🟡

Progesterone

Progesterone is produced mainly by the corpus luteum after ovulation. It prepares and supports the endometrium during the luteal phase.

🧬

Inhibin

Inhibin is produced by ovarian cells and contributes to regulation of FSH secretion.

4. Follicle-Stimulating Hormone

FSH is secreted by the anterior pituitary. At the beginning of the cycle, FSH supports recruitment and development of ovarian follicles.

🩺 Midwifery connection

Follicular development is important because the developing follicle contains the oocyte and produces hormones involved in preparing the reproductive system for ovulation.

5. Estrogen and the Endometrium

As the dominant ovarian follicle develops, estrogen production increases. Estrogen stimulates proliferation and thickening of the endometrium after menstruation.

Follicle develops → Estrogen increases → Endometrium proliferates

6. Ovulation

Ovulation is the release of a mature oocyte from an ovarian follicle.

Key event:

A sustained high level of estrogen from the dominant follicle can produce positive feedback that contributes to a rapid rise in LH, known as the LH surge. The LH surge triggers ovulation.

Dominant follicle → High estrogen → LH surge → Ovulation

7. Corpus Luteum

After ovulation, the ruptured follicle transforms into a temporary endocrine structure called the corpus luteum.

🟡 Main function

The corpus luteum produces progesterone and also produces some estrogen. These hormones help maintain a suitable endometrial environment during the luteal phase.

8. Phases of the Menstrual Cycle

🩸 Menstrual

Menstrual bleeding occurs as the functional endometrium is shed.

🌱 Follicular

Ovarian follicles develop and estrogen levels generally rise.

🥚 Ovulation

Release of an oocyte from the dominant ovarian follicle, triggered by the LH surge.

🌙 Luteal

The corpus luteum produces progesterone and supports the endometrium.

9. Menstrual Phase

Menstruation occurs when pregnancy has not been established and ovarian hormone levels fall following regression of the corpus luteum.

Corpus luteum regresses → Progesterone + estrogen fall → Endometrial support decreases → Menstruation

10. Follicular Phase

The follicular phase begins with menstruation and continues until ovulation. Ovarian follicles develop under the influence of FSH.

Usually one follicle becomes dominant. The dominant follicle produces increasing amounts of estrogen.

11. Ovulation Phase

Ovulation is the key event separating the follicular and luteal phases.

🩺 Fertility connection

The fertile window is related to the days surrounding ovulation because sperm can survive for several days in the female reproductive tract and the oocyte remains viable for a limited period after ovulation.

12. Luteal Phase

During the luteal phase, progesterone produced by the corpus luteum becomes the dominant ovarian hormone.

Progesterone supports the secretory transformation of the endometrium and prepares it for possible implantation.

13. Hormonal Feedback

Reproductive hormones regulate one another through feedback mechanisms.

1. Negative Feedback

Estrogen, progesterone and inhibin generally suppress excessive secretion of hypothalamic GnRH and pituitary gonadotropins during much of the cycle.

2. Positive Feedback

Near ovulation, sustained high estrogen levels can produce positive feedback that contributes to the LH surge.

14. Ovarian Cycle and Uterine Cycle

The menstrual cycle can be described from two connected perspectives: changes in the ovary and changes in the endometrium.

🥚 Ovarian Cycle

Includes follicular development, ovulation and the luteal phase.

🩸 Uterine Cycle

Includes menstrual, proliferative and secretory changes of the endometrium.

15. Simplified 28-Day Cycle

A 28-day cycle is a teaching model. Real menstrual cycles vary considerably between individuals.

Days 1–5: Menstruation

Menstrual bleeding occurs. Day 1 of bleeding is counted as day 1 of the menstrual cycle.

Days 1–13: Follicular Phase

Follicular development occurs while estrogen gradually increases.

Around Day 14: Ovulation

In a textbook 28-day cycle, ovulation is often estimated around day 14. Actual timing varies.

Days 15–28: Luteal Phase

The corpus luteum produces progesterone and supports the endometrium.

16. What Happens if Fertilization Occurs?

If sperm fertilizes the oocyte, the resulting embryo begins development and travels toward the uterus. Implantation can then occur if the developing conceptus successfully attaches to the endometrium.

Ovulation → Fertilization → Embryonic development → Implantation → Pregnancy continues

After implantation, trophoblastic tissue produces human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG), which supports continued corpus luteum function early in pregnancy.

17. Connection to Hormonal Contraception

Many hormonal contraceptive methods work partly by altering normal reproductive hormonal signaling.

💊 Important concept

Depending on the method, hormonal contraception may suppress ovulation, thicken cervical mucus and/or change the endometrium. The exact mechanism varies by contraceptive method.

18. Midwifery and Clinical Relevance

📅 Menstrual History

Menstrual history helps the midwife assess cycle patterns and can assist with estimating gestational age when pregnancy occurs.

🤰 Pregnancy Assessment

Understanding ovulation and implantation helps explain early pregnancy development and pregnancy testing.

🍼 Reproductive Counseling

Knowledge of the cycle helps with fertility education and contraceptive counseling.

🩺 Abnormal Bleeding

Understanding normal hormonal cycling helps clinicians recognize menstrual patterns that require further assessment.

19. Menstrual Changes That May Need Assessment

Menstrual patterns vary, but some changes can indicate the need for clinical assessment.

  • Very heavy menstrual bleeding.
  • Persistent irregular or unpredictable bleeding.
  • Very painful menstruation affecting normal activities.
  • Bleeding between periods that is new or unexplained.
  • Absent menstruation when pregnancy or another cause should be evaluated.
  • Bleeding after menopause.
⚠️ Clinical warning

Abnormal uterine bleeding has many possible causes. Assessment should consider pregnancy, age, symptoms, medications, contraception and other clinical factors.

20. Summary

🧠 Remember

GnRH comes from the hypothalamus.

FSH and LH are released from the anterior pituitary.

FSH supports ovarian follicle development.

The LH surge triggers ovulation.

Estrogen promotes endometrial proliferation.

Progesterone is mainly produced by the corpus luteum after ovulation.

Menstruation occurs when ovarian hormone levels fall after corpus luteum regression if pregnancy has not been established.

Ovulation is the release of an oocyte from the ovary.

The menstrual cycle is regulated by coordinated hormonal feedback.

🧠 Quick Review

1. What hormone is released by the hypothalamus to regulate the reproductive axis?
GnRH — gonadotropin-releasing hormone.
2. Which two hormones are released by the anterior pituitary?
FSH and LH.
3. What is the main role of FSH?
It supports development of ovarian follicles.
4. What triggers ovulation?
The LH surge triggers ovulation.
5. What hormone is dominant during the luteal phase?
Progesterone is the dominant ovarian hormone during the luteal phase.
6. What happens to the endometrium during the proliferative phase?
It grows and thickens under the influence of increasing estrogen.
7. What causes menstruation when pregnancy has not occurred?
Regression of the corpus luteum causes estrogen and progesterone levels to fall, leading to breakdown and shedding of the functional endometrium.
8. What is the corpus luteum?
It is the temporary endocrine structure formed from the ruptured ovarian follicle after ovulation.
9. Why is menstrual-cycle knowledge important in midwifery?
It supports menstrual assessment, fertility education, pregnancy assessment, contraception counseling and recognition of abnormal bleeding.
