The Digestive System

Learn how food is broken down, nutrients are absorbed, and waste is eliminated—and why good nutrition is especially important during pregnancy.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Define the digestive system.
  • Identify the major organs of the digestive tract.
  • Describe the functions of the accessory digestive organs.
  • Explain mechanical and chemical digestion.
  • Explain where major nutrients are absorbed.
  • Describe common digestive changes during pregnancy.
  • Explain the importance of nutrition in pregnancy.

1. What is the Digestive System?

The digestive system is responsible for taking in food, breaking it down into usable nutrients, absorbing those nutrients, and eliminating undigested waste.

Simple definition:

Digestion changes food into substances the body can absorb and use.

2. The Digestive Tract

The digestive tract is a continuous muscular tube extending from the mouth to the anus.

👄

Mouth

Food enters here. Chewing begins mechanical digestion and saliva begins chemical digestion.

🛤️

Esophagus

Moves swallowed food toward the stomach through coordinated muscular contractions.

🥣

Stomach

Stores food temporarily and mixes it with gastric secretions.

🌀

Small Intestine

Most digestion and nutrient absorption occur here.

💧

Large Intestine

Absorbs water and electrolytes and forms feces.

🚻

Rectum and Anus

Store and eliminate feces.

3. Pathway of Food

Mouth ↓ Pharynx ↓ Esophagus ↓ Stomach ↓ Small Intestine ↓ Large Intestine ↓ Rectum ↓ Anus

4. Accessory Digestive Organs

Some organs help digestion by producing or storing substances used in digestion, even though food does not pass directly through them.

Salivary Glands

Produce saliva that moistens food and contains enzymes that begin carbohydrate digestion.

Liver

Produces bile and performs many metabolic and detoxification functions.

Gallbladder

Stores and concentrates bile produced by the liver.

Pancreas

Produces digestive enzymes and bicarbonate that enter the small intestine.

5. Digestion

Digestion occurs through both mechanical and chemical processes.

Mechanical Digestion

Physical breakdown of food into smaller pieces, such as chewing and stomach mixing.

Chemical Digestion

Enzymes and digestive secretions break large food molecules into smaller molecules that can be absorbed.

6. Major Nutrients

Carbohydrates

Important source of energy. They are digested mainly into simple sugars such as glucose.

Proteins

Broken down into amino acids used for growth, repair and many body functions.

Fats

Provide energy and are important for cell membranes and absorption of fat-soluble vitamins.

Vitamins & Minerals

Support many physiological processes, including growth, blood formation and metabolism.

7. Nutrient Absorption

Most nutrient absorption occurs in the small intestine. Its inner surface contains folds and microscopic projections that greatly increase the area available for absorption.

Key point:

Small intestine = major site of nutrient absorption.

8. Large Intestine

The large intestine absorbs water and electrolytes and helps form and store feces before elimination.

🩺 Clinical connection

Reduced bowel movement can contribute to constipation, which is a common complaint during pregnancy.

9. Digestive Changes During Pregnancy

Pregnancy can produce several changes in gastrointestinal function.

Nausea and Vomiting

Nausea and vomiting are common, especially during early pregnancy.

Heartburn

Hormonal changes and pressure from the enlarging uterus can contribute to reflux symptoms.

Constipation

Hormonal effects and reduced gastrointestinal motility can contribute to constipation.

Increased Abdominal Pressure

The growing uterus changes the position and available space of abdominal organs.

10. Digestion and Nutrition in Pregnancy

Adequate nutrition is essential for maternal health, fetal growth and normal pregnancy development.

Important nutritional areas include:

Adequate energy, protein, iron, folate, calcium, iodine and other essential nutrients according to recommended antenatal guidance.

11. Nausea and Vomiting in Pregnancy

Mild nausea and vomiting can occur during pregnancy. However, severe or persistent vomiting may lead to dehydration, electrolyte abnormalities and inadequate nutritional intake.

⚠️ Clinical warning

Severe or persistent vomiting, inability to keep fluids down, reduced urine output, marked weakness or other concerning symptoms require clinical assessment.

12. Constipation in Pregnancy

Constipation may occur because of hormonal changes, reduced gastrointestinal motility, dietary factors and pressure from the growing uterus.

🩺 General supportive measures

Appropriate fluid intake, adequate dietary fibre, regular physical activity when medically appropriate, and following antenatal advice may help prevent constipation.

13. Summary

🍎 Remember

Mouth → Esophagus → Stomach → Small Intestine → Large Intestine → Rectum → Anus

Small intestine = major site of nutrient absorption.

Liver = produces bile.

Gallbladder = stores bile.

Pancreas = produces digestive enzymes and bicarbonate.

Pregnancy can cause nausea, reflux and constipation.

🧠 Quick Review

1. What is the main function of the digestive system?
To digest food, absorb nutrients and eliminate undigested waste.
2. Where does most nutrient absorption occur?
In the small intestine.
3. What is the function of the liver in digestion?
It produces bile and performs many other important metabolic functions.
4. What does the gallbladder do?
It stores and concentrates bile.
5. Name two common digestive complaints during pregnancy.
Nausea/vomiting, heartburn and constipation are common examples.
6. Why is nutrition important during pregnancy?
Adequate nutrients support maternal health, fetal growth and normal pregnancy development.
