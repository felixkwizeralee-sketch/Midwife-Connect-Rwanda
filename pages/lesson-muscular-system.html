The Muscular System

Learn the three types of muscle tissue and understand how muscles support movement, breathing, posture and the process of labour.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Define the muscular system.
  • Identify the three types of muscle tissue.
  • Differentiate skeletal, cardiac and smooth muscle.
  • Explain voluntary and involuntary muscle action.
  • Explain the role of the uterus in labour.
  • Describe the importance of pelvic floor muscles in midwifery.

1. What is the Muscular System?

The muscular system is made mainly of specialized muscle tissues that can contract and produce force.

Muscle contraction allows the body to move, maintain posture, pump blood and move substances through different organs.

Simple definition:

Muscle tissue is specialized tissue that can contract and generate force.

2. Three Types of Muscle

💪

Skeletal Muscle

Attached mainly to bones and responsible for voluntary body movement.

❤️

Cardiac Muscle

Found in the heart and contracts continuously to pump blood.

🌀

Smooth Muscle

Found in the walls of many internal organs and generally works involuntarily.

3. Comparing the Three Muscle Types

Type Main Location Control Example
Skeletal Attached to bones Mainly voluntary Biceps
Cardiac Heart Involuntary Myocardium
Smooth Internal organs Involuntary Uterus

4. Skeletal Muscle

Skeletal muscles are attached to bones and help produce movement. They are generally under conscious control.

Examples

  • Biceps
  • Triceps
  • Quadriceps
  • Muscles involved in posture and movement
🩺 Midwifery connection

A pregnant woman uses many skeletal muscles when walking, changing position, pushing and maintaining posture during labour.

5. Cardiac Muscle

Cardiac muscle forms the muscular wall of the heart. Its rhythmic contractions allow the heart to pump blood.

❤️ Clinical connection

The cardiovascular system depends on cardiac muscle contractions to maintain circulation to the mother and fetus.

6. Smooth Muscle

Smooth muscle is found in the walls of many hollow organs. It usually contracts involuntarily.

Examples

  • Uterus
  • Intestines
  • Blood vessels
  • Urinary bladder

7. Uterine Muscle and Labour

The muscular layer of the uterus is called the myometrium.

During labour, coordinated contractions of the myometrium contribute to cervical dilation and fetal descent.

🤰 Key Midwifery Point

The myometrium is made predominantly of smooth muscle and contracts involuntarily during labour.

What happens during a contraction?

The uterine muscle contracts and then relaxes. Repeated coordinated contractions help the cervix dilate and help move the fetus through the birth canal.

8. Pelvic Floor Muscles

The pelvic floor is a group of muscles and connective tissues that support structures within the pelvis.

Important for midwives:

The pelvic floor contributes to support of pelvic organs and plays an important role in continence, sexual function and childbirth.

9. Muscle Fatigue

Muscles may become fatigued when they are repeatedly or continuously active.

During prolonged physical activity or labour, adequate rest, hydration, nutrition and appropriate clinical monitoring are important.

10. Summary

🧠 Remember the 3 muscles

Skeletal → movement

Cardiac → heart pumping

Smooth → internal organs

Uterus = smooth muscle

🧠 Quick Review

1. Name the three types of muscle tissue.
Skeletal, cardiac and smooth muscle.
2. Which type of muscle is found in the heart?
Cardiac muscle.
3. Which type of muscle forms the myometrium?
Smooth muscle.
4. Is uterine muscle voluntary or involuntary?
Involuntary.
5. What is the main role of the myometrium during labour?
Coordinated uterine contractions contribute to cervical dilation and fetal descent.
6. Why are pelvic floor muscles important?
They help support pelvic organs and contribute to continence and childbirth.
