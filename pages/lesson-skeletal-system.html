The Skeletal System

Learn the major bones of the human body, their functions, and why the pelvis is especially important in midwifery and childbirth.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Define the skeletal system.
  • Describe major functions of bones.
  • Identify major divisions of the skeleton.
  • Identify important bones of the human body.
  • Describe the female pelvis.
  • Explain the importance of the pelvis in childbirth.

1. What is the Skeletal System?

The skeletal system is the body's framework. It consists mainly of bones and cartilage, together with joints and supporting connective tissues.

Simple definition:

The skeletal system provides the framework that supports and protects the body.

2. Functions of the Skeletal System

🏗️

Support

Bones provide a framework that supports the body and maintains its shape.

🛡️

Protection

Bones protect important organs such as the brain, heart and lungs.

🏃

Movement

Bones work with muscles and joints to allow movement.

🩸

Blood Cell Formation

Bone marrow produces important blood cells through hematopoiesis.

🧂

Mineral Storage

Bones store minerals, especially calcium and phosphorus.

⚖️

Body Balance

Bones contribute to mineral balance and other physiological functions.

3. Major Divisions of the Skeleton

The skeleton can be divided into two major parts:

🧍

Axial Skeleton

Includes the skull, vertebral column, ribs and sternum. It forms the central axis of the body.

🦵

Appendicular Skeleton

Includes the bones of the upper and lower limbs and the shoulder and pelvic girdles.

4. Major Bones to Know

Skull

Protects the brain and forms the structure of the head.

Vertebral Column

Supports the body and protects the spinal cord.

Ribs

Form the thoracic cage and help protect the heart and lungs.

Sternum

The breastbone located at the front of the thoracic cage.

Femur

The longest and strongest bone of the human body, located in the thigh.

Humerus

The major bone of the upper arm.

5. The Female Pelvis

The pelvis is one of the most important parts of the skeletal system for midwives because it forms the bony passage through which the fetus passes during vaginal birth.

🤰 Why is the pelvis important in childbirth?

The size and shape of the maternal pelvis can influence the passage of the fetal head and body during labour and birth.

Major bones of the pelvis

The bony pelvis includes the two hip bones, the sacrum and the coccyx.

🦴

Ilium

The broad, upper part of each hip bone.

🦴

Ischium

The lower and posterior part of the hip bone.

🦴

Pubis

The anterior portion of the hip bone.

🦴

Sacrum

A triangular bone forming the posterior part of the pelvis.

🦴

Coccyx

The small terminal part of the vertebral column below the sacrum.

6. The Pelvis and Labour

During labour, the fetal presenting part must pass through the maternal pelvis. Midwives therefore need to understand pelvic anatomy when assessing labour.

🩺 Clinical connection

Knowledge of the maternal pelvis helps the midwife understand fetal descent, engagement, station, position and the mechanism of labour.

Important pelvic regions discussed in obstetrics include the pelvic inlet, pelvic cavity and pelvic outlet.

7. Bone Marrow

Some bones contain bone marrow. Red bone marrow is responsible for producing blood cells.

Remember:

Red bone marrow → production of blood cells.

8. Summary

The skeletal system provides support, protection, movement and other important functions. For midwives, understanding the maternal pelvis is particularly important because it forms the bony birth passage.

🧠 Exam Memory

Skeleton = Support + Protection + Movement + Blood formation + Mineral storage

🧠 Quick Review

1. What is the main function of the skeleton?
It provides structural support and protection, while also contributing to movement and other physiological functions.
2. Name the two major divisions of the skeleton.
Axial skeleton and appendicular skeleton.
3. Which bone is the longest in the human body?
The femur.
4. Why is the pelvis important to midwives?
It forms the bony passage through which the fetus passes during vaginal birth.
5. Name three bones that form part of the pelvis.
Ilium, ischium and pubis. The sacrum and coccyx also form part of the bony pelvis.
