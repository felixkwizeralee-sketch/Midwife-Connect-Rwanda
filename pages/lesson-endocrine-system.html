The Endocrine System

Learn how hormones regulate growth, metabolism, reproduction, pregnancy, labour and breastfeeding.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Define the endocrine system.
  • Explain what hormones are.
  • Identify major endocrine glands.
  • Describe important hormones and their functions.
  • Explain the role of hormones in pregnancy.
  • Explain hormonal control of labour.
  • Explain the role of hormones in breastfeeding.
  • Recognize important endocrine connections in midwifery practice.

1. What is the Endocrine System?

The endocrine system is a group of glands and tissues that produce hormones. Hormones are chemical messengers released into the blood that act on target cells or organs.

Simple definition:

The endocrine system uses hormones to coordinate many functions of the body.

2. What are Hormones?

Hormones are chemical messengers produced by specialized cells or glands. They travel through the bloodstream or act locally to influence target tissues.

Endocrine gland → Hormone → Bloodstream → Target cell → Response

3. Major Endocrine Glands

🧠

Hypothalamus

Connects the nervous and endocrine systems and helps regulate pituitary hormone secretion, temperature, hunger and other homeostatic functions.

🎛️

Pituitary Gland

Produces and releases hormones that regulate growth, reproduction, thyroid function and adrenal activity. It is sometimes called the "master gland."

🦋

Thyroid Gland

Produces thyroid hormones that have major effects on metabolism, growth and development.

🦴

Parathyroid Glands

Produce parathyroid hormone, which helps regulate calcium and phosphate balance.

⚡

Adrenal Glands

Produce hormones including cortisol, aldosterone and adrenaline-related hormones.

🍬

Pancreas

Produces insulin and glucagon, which help regulate blood glucose.

🥚

Ovaries

Produce reproductive hormones including estrogen and progesterone.

🤰

Placenta

During pregnancy, the placenta produces important hormones that support pregnancy and prepare the body for birth and breastfeeding.

4. Pituitary Hormones

The pituitary gland has an important role in reproductive and endocrine regulation.

Follicle-Stimulating Hormone — FSH

FSH contributes to ovarian follicle development and reproductive function.

Luteinizing Hormone — LH

LH is involved in ovulation and formation of the corpus luteum.

Prolactin

Prolactin stimulates milk production after childbirth.

Oxytocin

Oxytocin is released from the posterior pituitary. It contributes to uterine contractions and milk ejection.

5. Thyroid Hormones

The thyroid gland produces thyroid hormones that influence metabolic activity, growth and development.

🩺 Midwifery connection

Thyroid disorders can affect pregnancy and maternal wellbeing. Appropriate clinical assessment and management are important when thyroid disease is suspected or already diagnosed.

6. Insulin and Glucagon

Insulin

Helps lower blood glucose by promoting glucose uptake and storage in appropriate tissues.

Glucagon

Helps raise blood glucose when glucose levels are low, partly by promoting release of glucose from stored sources.

🍬 Remember

Insulin generally decreases blood glucose, while glucagon generally increases blood glucose.

7. Reproductive Hormones

Estrogen

Estrogens are important reproductive hormones involved in development of female reproductive characteristics, the menstrual cycle and changes associated with pregnancy.

Progesterone

Progesterone supports the endometrium and has important roles in maintaining pregnancy and preparing the breasts for lactation.

Human Chorionic Gonadotropin — hCG

hCG is produced by placental trophoblastic tissue and can be detected by pregnancy tests. Early in pregnancy, it supports the corpus luteum so progesterone production can continue.

8. Hormones During Pregnancy

Pregnancy involves major endocrine changes. The placenta becomes an important endocrine organ and produces hormones that support the developing pregnancy.

hCG

Important in early pregnancy and commonly detected by pregnancy tests.

Estrogen

Supports growth of reproductive tissues and contributes to physiological changes of pregnancy.

Progesterone

Supports the uterine environment and contributes to maintenance of pregnancy.

Human Placental Lactogen

Contributes to maternal metabolic adaptations during pregnancy and helps prepare the breasts for lactation.

9. Hormones and Labour

Hormonal and mechanical processes work together during labour.

Oxytocin

Oxytocin promotes uterine contractions. Its release is associated with cervical stretching and other neuroendocrine mechanisms during labour.

Prostaglandins

Prostaglandins contribute to cervical ripening and uterine activity.

Cervical changes → Neuroendocrine signals → Oxytocin release → Uterine contractions → Further cervical stimulation

10. Hormones and Breastfeeding

Prolactin — Milk Production

Prolactin supports production of breast milk. Breast stimulation after birth contributes to prolactin release.

Oxytocin — Milk Ejection

Oxytocin causes contraction of myoepithelial cells around the milk-producing structures, helping milk move toward the nipple.

🤱 Midwifery connection

Early and effective breastfeeding stimulation can support the hormonal processes involved in milk production and milk ejection.

11. Adrenal Hormones

Cortisol

Cortisol participates in stress responses, metabolism and immune regulation.

Aldosterone

Aldosterone helps regulate sodium and potassium balance and contributes to blood volume and blood pressure regulation.

Adrenaline

Adrenaline contributes to the body's rapid response to stress by affecting cardiovascular and respiratory functions.

12. Hormonal Feedback

Many endocrine systems are controlled by feedback mechanisms. Negative feedback helps maintain hormone levels within an appropriate range.

Simple example:

When the level of a hormone or its effect becomes sufficient, signals can reduce further hormone production.

13. Why the Endocrine System Matters in Midwifery

🤰

Pregnancy

Hormones support implantation, pregnancy maintenance and maternal adaptation.

👶

Labour

Oxytocin and prostaglandins contribute to uterine activity and cervical changes.

🤱

Lactation

Prolactin and oxytocin are central to milk production and milk ejection.

🍬

Metabolism

Insulin, glucagon and thyroid hormones influence maternal metabolism and energy regulation.

14. Clinical Warning Signs

Endocrine-related symptoms may have many possible causes. Pregnant or postpartum women with significant symptoms should receive appropriate clinical assessment.

  • Severe unexplained weakness or fatigue.
  • Significant symptoms of abnormal blood glucose.
  • Symptoms suggesting thyroid dysfunction.
  • Excessive thirst and frequent urination with other concerning symptoms.
  • Altered consciousness or severe deterioration.
⚠️ Clinical warning

Endocrine symptoms should be interpreted together with history, examination and appropriate laboratory investigations rather than from a single symptom alone.

15. Summary

🧪 Remember

Endocrine system = glands + hormones + target tissues.

Pituitary hormones regulate many reproductive and endocrine functions.

Insulin generally lowers blood glucose.

Estrogen and progesterone are important reproductive hormones.

hCG is an important early pregnancy hormone.

Oxytocin contributes to uterine contractions and milk ejection.

Prolactin supports milk production.

The placenta is an important endocrine organ during pregnancy.

🧠 Quick Review

1. What is the endocrine system?
A system of glands and tissues that produce hormones to regulate body functions.
2. What are hormones?
Chemical messengers that act on target cells or tissues.
3. Which hormone is detected by most pregnancy tests?
Human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG).
4. Which hormone stimulates milk production?
Prolactin.
5. Which hormone is important for milk ejection?
Oxytocin.
6. Which hormone generally lowers blood glucose?
Insulin.
7. Name two hormones important during labour.
Oxytocin and prostaglandins.
8. Why is the placenta considered an endocrine organ?
Because it produces hormones that support pregnancy and maternal adaptation.
