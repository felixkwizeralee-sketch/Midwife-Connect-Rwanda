Introduction to Anatomy & Physiology

Before studying individual body systems, every student midwife needs to understand the basic concepts of anatomy and physiology.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Define anatomy.
  • Define physiology.
  • Explain the difference between anatomy and physiology.
  • Explain why anatomy and physiology are important in midwifery.
  • Describe the basic levels of organization of the human body.

1. What is Anatomy?

Anatomy is the study of the structure of the body and the relationships between its different parts.

Simple definition:

Anatomy = the study of body structure.

For example, when we study the uterus, we can examine its size, shape, position, layers and relationship with surrounding organs.

2. What is Physiology?

Physiology is the study of how the body and its parts function.

Simple definition:

Physiology = the study of body function.

For example, physiology helps us understand how the uterus contracts during labour, how the heart pumps blood and how the lungs exchange oxygen and carbon dioxide.

3. Anatomy vs Physiology

🧬 Anatomy

Focuses on body structure.

Example: The uterus has a muscular wall called the myometrium.

⚙️ Physiology

Focuses on body function.

Example: The myometrium contracts during labour.

💡 Remember:

Anatomy = structure
Physiology = function

4. Why Are They Important in Midwifery?

Midwives need a strong understanding of anatomy and physiology because pregnancy, labour, birth and postpartum changes involve many body systems.

Examples:

🤰 Understanding the uterus helps the midwife assess pregnancy and labour.

❤️ Understanding circulation helps the midwife recognize abnormal maternal vital signs.

🫁 Understanding respiration helps the midwife assess maternal and newborn breathing.

🧠 Understanding the nervous system helps the midwife assess neurological symptoms.

🩸 Understanding blood and circulation helps the midwife recognize and respond to bleeding.

5. Levels of Organization

The human body is organized from very small structures into increasingly complex structures.

1
Cell

The basic structural and functional unit of the body.

2
Tissue

A group of similar cells performing a common function.

3
Organ

A structure made of different tissues working together.

4
Organ System

A group of organs working together to perform major functions.

5
Organism

The complete living human being.

🧠 Exam tip

Remember the sequence:

Cell → Tissue → Organ → Organ System → Organism

6. Key Terms

Anatomy Physiology Cell Tissue Organ Organ System Organism

🧠 Quick Review

1. What is anatomy?
Anatomy is the study of body structure and the relationships between body parts.
2. What is physiology?
Physiology is the study of how the body and its parts function.
3. What is the correct sequence of body organization?
Cell → Tissue → Organ → Organ System → Organism.
4. Why is anatomy and physiology important to midwives?
They provide the foundation needed to understand normal body function and recognize abnormalities during pregnancy, labour, birth and postpartum care.
