The Urinary System

Learn how the kidneys form urine, regulate water and electrolytes, remove waste, and support important physiological functions during pregnancy.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Define the urinary system.
  • Identify the major organs of the urinary system.
  • Explain the basic functions of the kidneys.
  • Describe the nephron and its role in urine formation.
  • Explain filtration, reabsorption and secretion.
  • Describe the pathway of urine through the urinary tract.
  • Explain important urinary changes during pregnancy.
  • Recognize important urinary symptoms that need clinical attention.

1. What is the Urinary System?

The urinary system is a group of organs that removes metabolic waste from the blood and helps regulate the body's internal environment.

It also plays important roles in controlling water, electrolytes, acid-base balance and blood pressure.

Simple definition:

The urinary system removes waste from the body and helps maintain fluid and chemical balance.

2. Major Organs of the Urinary System

🫘

Kidneys

Filter blood, form urine and regulate fluid, electrolyte and acid-base balance.

🛣️

Ureters

Muscular tubes that transport urine from the kidneys to the bladder.

🫙

Urinary Bladder

Stores urine until it is eliminated.

🚻

Urethra

Carries urine from the bladder to the outside of the body.

3. Pathway of Urine

Kidneys ↓ Ureters ↓ Urinary Bladder ↓ Urethra ↓ Outside the Body

4. The Kidneys

The kidneys are paired organs located toward the back of the abdominal cavity. They receive a large blood supply so that blood can be continuously filtered.

Major functions of the kidneys

  • Remove metabolic waste.
  • Regulate water balance.
  • Regulate electrolytes.
  • Help maintain acid-base balance.
  • Contribute to blood pressure regulation.
  • Produce erythropoietin, which supports red blood cell production.
  • Activate vitamin D, contributing to calcium homeostasis and bone health.

5. The Nephron

The nephron is the functional unit of the kidney. Each kidney contains a very large number of nephrons.

Glomerulus

A network of capillaries where blood filtration begins.

Bowman's Capsule

Surrounds the glomerulus and collects the filtered fluid.

Renal Tubules

Modify the filtrate through reabsorption and secretion.

Collecting Duct

Receives tubular fluid and contributes to final urine concentration and transport.

6. How is Urine Formed?

Urine formation can be understood through three major processes.

1. Filtration

Blood is filtered at the glomerulus. Water and many small dissolved substances enter the nephron, while blood cells and most large proteins normally remain in the bloodstream.

2. Reabsorption

Useful substances such as much of the filtered water, glucose and electrolytes are returned from the tubules to the blood according to the body's needs.

3. Secretion

Certain substances are moved from the blood into the renal tubules for elimination.

Filtration → Reabsorption → Secretion → Urine

7. Fluid and Electrolyte Balance

The kidneys continuously adjust the amount of water and electrolytes retained or excreted.

Important electrolytes include:

Sodium, potassium, chloride, calcium and phosphate.

Proper regulation is essential for normal nerve, muscle and cardiovascular function.

8. Hormonal Regulation of Water

Antidiuretic hormone (ADH) helps the kidneys conserve water by increasing water reabsorption in appropriate parts of the renal tubules and collecting ducts.

Remember:

More ADH effect → more water retained → more concentrated urine.

Less ADH effect → more water excreted → more dilute urine.

9. Urinary Changes During Pregnancy

Pregnancy causes physiological changes in the urinary system because of increased blood flow, hormonal changes and the enlarging uterus.

Increased Renal Blood Flow

Blood flow through the kidneys increases during pregnancy.

Increased GFR

Glomerular filtration rate normally increases during pregnancy.

Frequent Urination

Increased urinary frequency may occur because of physiological and mechanical changes.

Ureter Changes

Hormonal and mechanical effects can cause dilation of the urinary collecting system.

10. Urine Examination in Antenatal Care

Urine testing can provide useful information during antenatal care and assessment.

🩺 Common findings assessed may include:

Protein, glucose, ketones, blood, leukocytes, nitrites and other findings depending on the test being used.

Interpretation should always consider the woman's symptoms, clinical condition and the specific test method used.

11. Urinary Tract Infection (UTI)

A urinary tract infection occurs when microorganisms infect part of the urinary tract.

Possible symptoms

  • Pain or burning during urination.
  • Increased urinary frequency or urgency.
  • Lower abdominal discomfort.
  • Cloudy or unusual-smelling urine may occur.
⚠️ Important in pregnancy

Pregnant women with suspected urinary infection should receive appropriate clinical assessment because infection can progress and may be associated with pregnancy complications.

12. Possible Signs of Kidney Infection

Infection involving the kidneys may cause more severe symptoms.

  • Fever or chills.
  • Flank or back pain.
  • Nausea or vomiting.
  • Urinary symptoms.
🚨 Clinical warning

Fever with flank pain, systemic illness or suspected kidney infection during pregnancy requires prompt medical assessment.

13. Protein in Urine

Protein detected in urine can have several causes. In pregnancy, significant proteinuria may be relevant when assessing hypertensive disorders of pregnancy.

🩺 Midwifery connection

Urine protein should not be interpreted alone. Blood pressure, symptoms and other clinical findings must also be considered when assessing a pregnant woman.

14. Summary

💧 Remember

Kidneys → Ureters → Bladder → Urethra

Nephron = functional unit of the kidney.

Filtration → Reabsorption → Secretion → Urine.

Kidneys regulate water, electrolytes and acid-base balance.

Pregnancy increases renal blood flow and GFR.

Urinary symptoms during pregnancy deserve appropriate clinical assessment.

🧠 Quick Review

1. What is the main functional unit of the kidney?
The nephron.
2. Name the four major organs of the urinary system.
Kidneys, ureters, urinary bladder and urethra.
3. Where does filtration begin?
At the glomerulus.
4. What is reabsorption?
Movement of useful substances from the renal tubules back into the blood.
5. What hormone helps the body conserve water?
Antidiuretic hormone (ADH).
6. Why is urinary infection important during pregnancy?
Infection may become more serious and requires appropriate assessment and treatment.
