The Cardiovascular System

Learn how the heart and blood vessels circulate blood, oxygen and nutrients throughout the body, and understand why cardiovascular knowledge is essential in midwifery.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Define the cardiovascular system.
  • Identify the main components of the cardiovascular system.
  • Describe the four chambers of the heart.
  • Identify the major blood vessels.
  • Explain pulmonary and systemic circulation.
  • Explain the meaning of pulse and blood pressure.
  • Describe important cardiovascular changes during pregnancy.

1. What is the Cardiovascular System?

The cardiovascular system is the body's transport system. It circulates blood through the heart and blood vessels.

Blood carries oxygen, nutrients, hormones and other substances to tissues and transports carbon dioxide and metabolic waste away from them.

Simple definition:

Cardiovascular system = Heart + Blood + Blood vessels.

2. Main Components

❤️

Heart

The muscular pump that moves blood through the circulation.

🩸

Blood

Transports oxygen, nutrients, hormones, waste products and immune components.

🔴

Arteries

Carry blood away from the heart.

🔵

Veins

Carry blood toward the heart.

3. The Heart

The heart is a muscular organ located in the thoracic cavity between the lungs.

It continuously pumps blood to the lungs and the rest of the body.

Four chambers of the heart

Right Atrium

Receives deoxygenated blood returning from the systemic circulation.

Right Ventricle

Pumps deoxygenated blood toward the lungs through the pulmonary artery.

Left Atrium

Receives oxygenated blood returning from the lungs through the pulmonary veins.

Left Ventricle

Pumps oxygenated blood into the systemic circulation through the aorta.

4. Blood Flow Through the Heart

Body ↓ Superior & Inferior Vena Cava ↓ Right Atrium ↓ Right Ventricle ↓ Pulmonary Artery ↓ Lungs ↓ Pulmonary Veins ↓ Left Atrium ↓ Left Ventricle ↓ Aorta ↓ Body

5. Blood Vessels

Arteries

Carry blood away from the heart. Most arteries carry oxygen-rich blood, although the pulmonary arteries carry oxygen-poor blood.

Veins

Carry blood toward the heart. Most veins carry oxygen-poor blood, although pulmonary veins carry oxygen-rich blood.

Capillaries

Tiny vessels where exchange of oxygen, nutrients and waste products occurs between blood and tissues.

6. Pulmonary and Systemic Circulation

Pulmonary Circulation

Pulmonary circulation carries blood between the heart and lungs.

Systemic Circulation

Systemic circulation carries blood between the heart and the tissues of the body.

🧠 Remember:

Pulmonary = Heart ↔ Lungs

Systemic = Heart ↔ Body

7. Pulse

A pulse is the rhythmic expansion of an artery caused by the heartbeat.

Common sites for assessing a pulse include the radial artery at the wrist and the carotid artery in the neck.

🩺 Midwifery connection

Maternal pulse is one of the important vital signs assessed during antenatal care, labour and postpartum care.

8. Blood Pressure

Blood pressure is the force exerted by circulating blood against the walls of blood vessels.

Systolic pressure

The pressure during ventricular contraction.

Diastolic pressure

The pressure during ventricular relaxation.

Example:

In a blood pressure reading of 120/80 mmHg, 120 represents systolic pressure and 80 represents diastolic pressure.

9. Cardiovascular Changes During Pregnancy

Pregnancy causes important physiological changes in the cardiovascular system to support the mother and developing fetus.

Increased Blood Volume

Maternal blood volume increases during pregnancy.

Increased Cardiac Output

The amount of blood pumped by the heart each minute generally increases during pregnancy.

Increased Heart Rate

Maternal resting heart rate commonly increases during pregnancy.

Circulatory Adaptation

Blood vessels and circulation adapt to meet increased maternal and fetal demands.

10. Fetal Circulation

Before birth, the fetus receives oxygen and nutrients through the placenta rather than through the lungs.

Fetal circulation contains specialized pathways that help direct blood around the developing lungs and liver.

🤰 Important structures

Umbilical vein, umbilical arteries, ductus venosus, foramen ovale and ductus arteriosus are important structures in fetal circulation.

11. Why This Matters in Midwifery

Midwives regularly assess cardiovascular function throughout pregnancy, labour and the postpartum period.

Important assessments include:

Blood pressure, pulse, respiratory status, signs of bleeding, peripheral perfusion and overall maternal condition.

⚠️ Clinical warning

Significant changes in maternal blood pressure, pulse, level of consciousness, breathing or bleeding require prompt clinical assessment and appropriate escalation according to local protocols.

12. Summary

❤️ Remember

Heart = Pump

Arteries = Away from heart

Veins = Toward heart

Capillaries = Exchange

Pulmonary = Heart ↔ Lungs

Systemic = Heart ↔ Body

🧠 Quick Review

1. What are the three main components of the cardiovascular system?
Heart, blood and blood vessels.
2. Which chamber pumps blood to the lungs?
The right ventricle.
3. Which chamber pumps blood to the body?
The left ventricle.
4. What is the difference between pulmonary and systemic circulation?
Pulmonary circulation is between the heart and lungs, while systemic circulation is between the heart and the rest of the body.
5. What does systolic blood pressure represent?
The pressure during ventricular contraction.
6. Why is cardiovascular knowledge important in midwifery?
Because cardiovascular assessment is essential for monitoring maternal and fetal wellbeing during pregnancy, labour and postpartum care.
