The Nervous System

Learn how the brain, spinal cord and nerves control communication, sensation, movement and important functions of the human body.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Define the nervous system.
  • Identify the major divisions of the nervous system.
  • Describe the functions of the brain and spinal cord.
  • Explain the role of nerves.
  • Differentiate the somatic and autonomic nervous systems.
  • Explain the relationship between the nervous system, pain and labour.

1. What is the Nervous System?

The nervous system is the body's major communication and control system. It receives information, processes it and coordinates appropriate responses.

Simple definition:

The nervous system detects information, processes it and helps the body respond.

2. Main Functions

👁️

Sensory Input

Detects changes inside and outside the body.

🧠

Integration

Processes and interprets information.

⚡

Motor Response

Sends signals that produce appropriate responses.

❤️

Regulation

Helps regulate many body functions and maintain internal balance.

3. Major Divisions

🧠

Central Nervous System

Consists of the brain and spinal cord.

🔌

Peripheral Nervous System

Includes nerves and ganglia outside the brain and spinal cord.

4. The Brain

The brain is the main control center of the nervous system. It receives and processes information and coordinates many body functions.

Important brain regions

Cerebrum

Involved in conscious thought, memory, sensation and voluntary movement.

Cerebellum

Helps coordinate movement, balance and posture.

Brainstem

Contains important centers involved in vital functions such as breathing and heart regulation.

5. Spinal Cord

The spinal cord is a long structure extending from the brain through the vertebral canal.

It carries nerve signals between the brain and much of the body and participates in reflexes.

Remember:

Brain + spinal cord = Central Nervous System.

6. Peripheral Nerves

Peripheral nerves connect the central nervous system with tissues throughout the body.

They can carry sensory information toward the central nervous system and motor information away from it.

7. Somatic Nervous System

The somatic nervous system is associated mainly with conscious sensation and voluntary control of skeletal muscles.

Example:

Choosing to move your arm or walk involves somatic motor control.

8. Autonomic Nervous System

The autonomic nervous system regulates many involuntary functions such as heart rate, digestion and blood vessel tone.

Sympathetic

Supports the body's response to stress or increased demand.

Parasympathetic

Generally supports rest, digestion and recovery functions.

9. Nervous System and Pain

Pain is a sensory experience involving detection, transmission and processing of signals by the nervous system.

🤰 Midwifery connection

During labour, sensory signals associated with uterine contractions and cervical changes are transmitted through nerves and processed by the nervous system.

10. Nervous System and Labour

The nervous system interacts with hormonal and other physiological systems during labour.

Maternal emotional state, fear, anxiety, pain and environmental factors can influence the experience of labour and the body's stress responses.

🩺 Midwifery practice

Calm communication, emotional support, appropriate pain-management options and respectful maternity care can help a woman cope with labour.

11. Reflex Action

A reflex is a rapid, automatic response to a stimulus. Some reflex pathways can be coordinated through the spinal cord without requiring conscious processing first.

Example:

Quickly withdrawing your hand after touching something hot is a common example of a protective reflex.

12. Summary

🧠 Remember

CNS = Brain + Spinal Cord

PNS = Nerves outside the CNS

Somatic = Mainly voluntary control

Autonomic = Involuntary regulation

🧠 Quick Review

1. What are the two main divisions of the nervous system?
Central nervous system and peripheral nervous system.
2. What structures form the CNS?
The brain and spinal cord.
3. What is the main role of the spinal cord?
It carries signals between the brain and much of the body and participates in reflexes.
4. Which system mainly controls voluntary skeletal muscle movement?
The somatic nervous system.
5. Which nervous system regulates many involuntary functions?
The autonomic nervous system.
6. Why is the nervous system important during labour?
It processes sensory information, including signals associated with contractions and pain, and coordinates many responses to the changing conditions of labour.
