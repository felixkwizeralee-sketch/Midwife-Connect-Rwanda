Anatomical Position & Directional Terms

Learn the standard body position and the anatomical terms used by healthcare professionals to describe the location and relationship of body structures.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Describe the standard anatomical position.
  • Explain common anatomical directional terms.
  • Distinguish between anterior and posterior.
  • Distinguish between superior and inferior.
  • Distinguish between medial and lateral.
  • Explain proximal and distal.
  • Apply anatomical terms to midwifery situations.

1. Anatomical Position

The anatomical position is the standard reference position used by healthcare professionals when describing the location of body structures.

A person in anatomical position is:

  • Standing upright.
  • Facing forward.
  • Head and eyes facing forward.
  • Arms positioned at the sides.
  • Palms facing forward.
  • Feet directed forward.
🧍
🧠 Important:

Anatomical directions are described from the person's anatomical position, not from the observer's left or right.

2. Anterior and Posterior

Anterior
Anterior = toward the front

Example: The sternum is anterior to the heart.

Posterior
Posterior = toward the back

Example: The vertebral column is posterior to the heart.

🤰 Midwifery example:

The pregnant uterus is generally anterior to the sacrum. In a clinical description, anatomical terms help healthcare providers communicate precisely.

3. Superior and Inferior

Superior
Superior = toward the head

Example: The head is superior to the chest.

Inferior
Inferior = toward the feet

Example: The feet are inferior to the abdomen.

4. Medial and Lateral

Medial
Medial = toward the midline

Example: The nose is medial to the eyes.

Lateral
Lateral = away from the midline

Example: The arms are lateral to the chest.

5. Proximal and Distal

These terms are especially useful when describing structures of the limbs.

Proximal
Proximal = closer to the point of attachment

Example: The elbow is proximal to the wrist.

Distal
Distal = farther from the point of attachment

Example: The fingers are distal to the elbow.

6. Superficial and Deep

Superficial
Superficial = closer to the body surface

Example: The skin is superficial to the muscles.

Deep
Deep = farther from the body surface

Example: Bones are generally deeper than the skin.

7. Clinical Application in Midwifery

Anatomical terminology allows midwives and other healthcare professionals to communicate clearly when documenting assessments and providing care.

🩺 Example:

Instead of saying "the wound is near the arm," a healthcare professional can describe its exact anatomical location using terms such as proximal, distal, medial or lateral.

Pregnancy example

The uterus enlarges superiorly from the pelvis during pregnancy. The growing uterus can therefore move upward toward the abdominal cavity.

Fetal position example

During obstetric assessment, terms such as anterior, posterior, left and right are used to describe fetal position and presentation.

8. Quick Summary

Anterior → toward the front

Posterior → toward the back

Superior → toward the head

Inferior → toward the feet

Medial → toward the midline

Lateral → away from the midline

Proximal → closer to attachment

Distal → farther from attachment

Superficial → closer to surface

Deep → farther from surface

🧠 Quick Review

1. What is the anatomical position?
A standard reference position in which the person stands upright, faces forward, keeps the arms at the sides and has the palms facing forward.
2. What does anterior mean?
Toward the front of the body.
3. What does posterior mean?
Toward the back of the body.
4. The nose is ______ to the eyes.
Medial.
5. The wrist is ______ to the elbow.
Distal.
6. The skin is ______ to the muscles.
Superficial.
